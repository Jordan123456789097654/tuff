Section 1: Biogeochemical Cycles & Nitrogen Fixation

1.1 Analysis Prompt: Nitrogen is an essential limiting nutrient in terrestrial ecosystems. Atmospheric nitrogen (N2) is chemically unreactive due to its strong triple covalent bond. Describe the biological pathway by which Rhizobium bacteria in legume root nodules convert atmospheric nitrogen into bioavailable ammonium (NH4+), and explain how subsequent nitrification by Nitrosomonas produces nitrates (NO3-) accessible by primary producers.

Student Response Notes:

Biological fixation occurs via the nitrogenase enzyme complex, reducing N2 to NH3 in anaerobic root microenvironments. Nitrification proceeds through two distinct obligate aerobic oxidation steps: (1) 2NH4+ + 3O2 → 2NO2- + 4H+ + 2H2O, followed by (2) 2NO2- + O2 → 2NO3-.

Section 3: Anthropogenic Impact & Watershed Conservation

3.1 Case Evaluation: Eutrophication in coastal estuaries is frequently exacerbated by excessive synthetic fertilizer runoff. Propose two agricultural mitigation strategies (e.g., riparian buffer zones, conservation tillage) and evaluate their comparative effectiveness in reducing dissolved phosphate concentrations.

Student Response Notes:

1. Riparian buffer strips consisting of native perennial vegetation filter surface runoff and uptake dissolved phosphates through root mass filtration before reaching surface waterways.

2. No-till agriculture preserves soil macro-pore structure, preventing fine particulate soil detachment and curbing phosphate-adsorbed sediment erosion.
