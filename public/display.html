Unit 4 Reading Assignment: Ecosystems & Environmental Biomes Module: 4.3 Terrestrial Nutrient Cycling

Chapter 4: Energetics of Primary Productivity & Energy Transfer in Trophic Pyramids

In any terrestrial ecosystem, energy begins with solar radiation captured by autotrophs through photosynthesis. Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) represents the total rate at which solar energy is converted into organic matter by primary producers over a given time interval. However, a significant fraction of this assimilated energy is immediately consumed by the producers themselves to fuel cellular respiration (\(R\)).

The remaining organic biomass available to heterotrophs in subsequent trophic levels is designated as Net Primary Productivity (NPP), governed by the fundamental ecological equation:

$$\text{NPP} = \text{GPP} - R_{\text{autotroph}}$$

Because energy transfer between successive trophic levels (from primary producers to primary consumers, secondary consumers, and apex predators) operates at an average ecological efficiency of approximately 10% (governed by Lindeman's Efficiency), substantial metabolic heat loss occurs at each juncture in accordance with the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

📚 Key Reading Concept: High NPP ecosystems include tropical rainforests, coral reefs, and estuaries, while arid deserts and open oceans exhibit lower biomass accumulation per square meter.
